01 · Logo

simply sosan, with the sprout

Space and size

  • Clear space: leave at least the height of the letter "o" empty on every side of the logo.
  • Smallest size: the stacked logo no narrower than 90px on screen (2.5 cm in print), the one-line logo no narrower than 140px, the sprout alone no smaller than 16px. Below that, use the sprout.
  • Use SVG wherever it's accepted; it stays sharp at any size. PNG is for apps that won't take SVG.

Do

  • Use the files here as they are.
  • Keep the logo lowercase: "simply sosan". In sentences, the name is written "Simply Sosan".
  • Put the ink logo on the light ground or the tint, and the white logo on the ink, the deep green or a photo's dark, plain area.

Don't

  • Retype "simply sosan" in a font. Always use the logo files.
  • Recolour, stretch, rotate or outline it, or add a shadow.
  • Separate the sprout from the letters, or move it, in the wordmark.
  • Place it on a busy photo or a mid-tone background where it disappears.

Text that's easy to read

Many readers are over 40, so contrast matters. These pairs meet the accessibility standard for text (4.5:1 or better). The sprout green on the light ground scores 1.8:1, which is why it's never used for words there.

  • Body text at 17px or larger on screen, 11pt or larger in print. Larger is kinder to tired eyes.
  • Headlines in sentence case ("Free guides, recipes and tips"), not Title Case or ALL CAPS.
  • Capitals only for short labels set in Nunito Sans extra bold.
04 · Voice

A knowledgeable friend, not a lecture

Sosan writes the way she talks on the radio: warm, practical and plain. She has 20 years of training behind her, but she never makes anyone feel behind.

Sounds like Simply Sosan

  • "Calcium alone isn't enough to protect your bones."
  • "Bring what you read, or what AI told you, to the live Q&A."
  • "A 10-day reset for blood sugar, sleep and energy."
  • Specific numbers and real steps: "10 minutes", "after 40", "three meals in 30 minutes".

Doesn't

  • Promises results: "Lower blood sugar in 10 days", "reverse diabetes".
  • Tells people they won't need their medication. Always: "talk to your doctor".
  • Fear or guilt: "before it's too late", "you're poisoning your family".
  • Jargon without a plain explanation next to it.
  • Two languages, equal care. English first, then Chinese, or a language switch, as on the site. Chinese in Traditional characters, written for a Cantonese reader.
  • Check that both languages say the same thing. A translation must not make a stronger health claim than the English does.
  • Canadian spelling in English: colour, centre, fibre, favourite.
  • Every health page carries the line: "Education only, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before changing any medication."
05 · Photography

Real, warm and at home

  • Sosan herself, in natural light, at home or in the kitchen. People join a person, so she should be in most images.
  • Real food, shot from above or at the counter, colourful and simple enough to cook on a weeknight.
  • Leave room for words: a plain wall, a counter or sky where a headline can sit.
  • No stock models in white lab coats, and no before-and-after body photos.
  • Ask before posting anyone else's face, including members at events.
